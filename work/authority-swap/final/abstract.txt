A factual answer can survive one misleading prompt while failing to distinguish evidence from a speaker's confidence. We tested whether claimed expertise changes false switching when the proposed wrong answer and its rationale are held fixed. The study used 85 factual questions, two answer orders for baseline eligibility, three balanced wording sets, and five models. Each eligible question received expert or ordinary role cues crossed with assertive or tentative tone, plus neutral reconsideration and a bare counterclaim. All results are descriptive following post-pilot protocol decisions. Across the three hosted models, paired expert-minus-ordinary differences ranged from -0.6 to +0.6 percentage points. Conservative 95% bounds ranged from ±4.7 to ±6.9 points and included zero. Each local model qualified on 26 questions. Qwen2.5-1.5B abstained on every core follow-up, while SmolLM2 switched on 11 of 26 neutral prompts. Exploratory repetition produced eight wrong replies in 104 local-Qwen branch histories by the third turn. In a separate correction arm with scripted wrong answers, hosted models accepted 44–46 of 47–48 usable bare corrections and all 48 evidence-backed corrections per model. These results bound the expertise effect within this factual task and show why false switching must be reported alongside abstention, neutral drift and correction uptake.
