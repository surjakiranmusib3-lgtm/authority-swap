## Proposed contribution and question

**Contribution frame:** Replicate the known vulnerability of initially correct answers to misleading follow-up pressure, then extend it with a controlled **claimed expertise × tone** comparison. The proposed false answer and factual rationale stay identical in expert/ordinary and assertive/tentative branches; only the speaker cue and strength of assertion change. Separate verified corrections address the possibility that apparent resistance is mere stubbornness. This does **not** claim the first authority, tone, or sycophancy study.

**Primary question, pending protocol freeze:** On source-verified two-option factual items answered correctly in **both A/B answer orders**, how much does claiming expertise change the probability of adopting the same keyed wrong answer, averaged across assertive and tentative wording, for each specified local model? The interaction with tone is secondary. The comparison is paired by question. Its eventual claims must be limited to the tested **1.5B–1.7B quantized local checkpoints**, prompts, and eligible items; no broad claim about all LLMs or evidence use follows automatically.

The literature gives competing expectations: Wang and Yang suggest weak framing effects in their settings; Joswin, SWAY and some SycoBench/Wavering comparisons suggest meaningful effects; PARROT raises a specific Qwen ceiling risk. Preserve both pilot **floor** and **ceiling** gates. Do not choose the result direction in advance or change the model after seeing main outcomes.
